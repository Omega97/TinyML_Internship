# 7 Conclusion and Future Work

## 7.1 Summary of Contributions

<span style="color: #808080;">[Core Contributions]</span> This thesis set out to bridge the gap between the theoretical capacity of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures and the strict computational constraints of on-device chess evaluation functions. By moving away from traditional, handcrafted bucketing based on piece count, we explored data-driven routing mechanisms capable of unlocking deeper expert specialization. The primary contribution is the formalization of the "routing gap" in NNUE architectures: the fundamental dichotomy between the feature space that is easily dispatchable at inference time (L1 activations) and the feature space that contains the true specialization signal (sample gradients). 

<span style="color: #808080;">[Empirical Insights]</span> Through extensive empirical evaluation, we established that while a strong reference base model leaves little room for single-stage MoE improvements, the underlying experts possess significant latent capacity. Our oracle upper bounds proved that if routed perfectly, the experts could achieve substantial cross-entropy reductions. Furthermore, we demonstrated that when applied to a slightly weaker base architecture, L1-clustered hard MoE variants could indeed outperform the baseline, provided the experts were given sufficient training data. This confirmed that the failure of MoE to beat the strong base was not a lack of capacity, but a failure of the inference-time routing signal to recover the optimal expert assignment.

<span style="color: #808080;">[Optimization Breakthroughs]</span> A critical secondary contribution was the identification and resolution of the optimization instabilities inherent in fine-tuning multiple expert heads. By transitioning to the Rprop optimizer and implementing a dynamic learning rate adjustment that steps back and slows down when the loss increases, we achieved a highly stable and reliable fine-tuning phase. This optimization trick was instrumental in allowing the experts to properly specialize without collapsing or overfitting, unlocking the first reproducible routing gains in our experimental battery.

<span style="color: #808080;">[The KITSUNE Paradigm]</span> To resolve the routing gap without sacrificing inference efficiency, we proposed KITSUNE, a novel two-stage routing framework. By decoupling the geometric partitioning of the L1 space into highly accurate micro-buckets from the offline, gradient-based stitching of those buckets into macro-experts, KITSUNE allows a strictly linear dispatcher to express arbitrarily non-convex gradient manifolds. While the full empirical validation of KITSUNE across all hyperparameter configurations is still ongoing, early results confirm that this two-stage approach successfully retains the actionable gradient signal while maintaining the near-perfect dispatchability of the L1 space, offering a measurable improvement over standard single-stage routing.

## 7.2 Future Work

<span style="color: #808080;">[Finalizing KITSUNE]</span> The immediate next step is to complete the exhaustive hyperparameter sweep for the KITSUNE architecture, specifically optimizing the ratio between the number of micro-buckets and macro-experts to balance gradient purity and dispatcher accuracy. Furthermore, we plan to implement constrained agglomerative clustering during the offline stitching phase to guarantee strict load balancing across all macro-experts, ensuring that no single expert becomes a bottleneck or remains underutilized. Once fully tuned, a direct comparison of the final KITSUNE MoE against the strong reference base will be conducted to quantify the exact cross-entropy and mean absolute error improvements.

<span style="color: #808080;">[Engine Integration]</span> While cross-entropy and MAE are excellent proxies for evaluation quality, they do not perfectly correlate with playing strength in a full search engine. Future work must involve integrating the KITSUNE dispatcher and the specialized expert heads directly into the C++ search engine. This will require optimizing the sparse matrix operations for the specific instruction sets of modern CPUs and measuring the actual nodes-per-second (NPS) overhead. Ultimately, the true value of the architecture will be validated through Elo rating tournaments against both the baseline NNUE and existing handcrafted bucket implementations.

<span style="color: #808080;">[KITSUNE]</span> The core premise of KITSUNE—decoupling a trivially dispatchable geometric space from a complex, signal-rich optimization space—is highly applicable beyond chess evaluation. We intend to explore the application of this two-stage routing paradigm to other domains that rely on shared-trunk, multi-head architectures, such as multi-task learning networks, recommendation systems, and autonomous driving models. In these contexts, the intermediate trunk activations could serve as the micro-bucketing space, while task-specific gradients could dictate the macro-expert stitching, potentially offering a highly efficient alternative to soft-gated MoE layers in large language models.

## 7.3 Closing Remarks

<span style="color: #808080;">[Final Takeaway]</span> The pursuit of highly specialized evaluation functions for constrained environments often forces a compromise between model capacity and inference speed. This thesis demonstrates that this compromise is not absolute. By rigorously analyzing the geometry of the routing space and introducing a two-stage, offline-computed dispatching mechanism, we can extract the deep specialization benefits of Mixture-of-Experts without incurring the computational penalties of dynamic, non-linear routing at runtime. Data-driven, gradient-informed partitions can successfully replace handcrafted heuristics, paving the way for a new generation of evaluation functions that are both mathematically optimal and practically deployable on the most constrained hardware.
