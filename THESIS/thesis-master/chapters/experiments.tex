\subsection{Base NNUE}\label{sec:res-base}

We also tested the soft cross-entropy of the base NNUE model as a function of the size of the dataset, from 50k samples all the way to 105 million. The model has a \(2\times 128\)-neuron accumulator layer and a 256-neuron L2 layer, which lies on the larger side of the models we trained.

\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\footnotesize,breaklines=true]
python3.12 -u scripts/train_nnue-gpu.py --epochs 100 --lr 0.01 --lr-end 0.001 --hidden-dim 128 --hidden2-dim 256 --test-fraction 0.01 --batch-size 10000 --batches-per-epoch 512 --train-val-subset-size 10000 --run-name dual_h128_H256_e100_bpe512_bs10000 --plot plots/dual_h128_H256_e100_bpe512_bs10000.png
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{variable_dataset_size_ce_64.png}
\caption{Soft cross-entropy of a smaller base NNUE as a function of training-set size.}

\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{variable_dataset_size_ce_256.png}
\caption{Soft cross-entropy of the \(W=128\), \(H=256\) base NNUE as a function of training-set size.}
\label{fig:nnue-ce-vs-size-256}
\end{figure}

We found that, below 7 million positions, the model clearly overfits the training set. On the other hand, above 7 million positions no overfitting is visible. These figures are to be kept in mind when partitioning a dataset for the MoE, as the per-expert data should never fall below this threshold.
